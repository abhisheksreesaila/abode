C2 · Milestone lanes, live: master, detail, talk

night · milestones over tickets · voice

One row per workspace, and the row is its plan, packed as squares. Each square is a ticket, and the squares sit in clusters, one cluster per milestone, from the start dot to the goal ◎. The current milestone is outlined, so you see status and structure at once without labels. Hover a square or a cluster for that milestone, or flip the toggle to Labels to see names on the track. The master stays narrow so the detail can be wide. A workspace with more than one thread (a fresh start, a perf pass) gets a sub-row per thread, each with its own track. The only words on a row are one status (the phase, or "needs you" / "red") and a pace arrow. Decisions, what's happening now, and the plan all live in the detail. A talk box stays open on the master (to all workspaces) and at the bottom of the detail (to that workspace) on every tab. Press / to start typing. Click a row and the detail opens beside it. Plan opens with "where it's heading" (the labelled track, milestones left, and roughly how many sessions to the goal at the current pace), then the same track drawn vertically: where it started, what each milestone achieved and how many sessions it took, where it is now, and what's left. Add milestones in between, rename them, or recalibrate the goal. Sessions holds the summaries. Jam is the chat. Milestones span sessions, and tickets stay out of sight. Talk or type into the bar any time. Flip on Passive and it stays quiet, speaking up only when a milestone lands or something needs you.

Cons to look into

Milestone % is a guessThe fill comes from tickets done ÷ tickets planned, so it jumps when the planner adds tickets mid-milestone. Decide whether you want that honesty or a smoother "confidence" number.
Pace needs a baseline"Accelerating" only means something against a history, like milestones per session or hours per milestone. The first week of a new thread won't have one, so it should say "new" until it does.
Thread sprawlIf threads are cheap, a workspace grows five sub-rows. Cap visible threads at two and fold the rest, or merge a thread into the main track once its goal lands.
Who owns the milestones?You set them, but agents will want to split or add some. Agent edits should show up as a suggestion ("add 'Share links'?") rather than silently changing your plan.
Voice misroutesWorkspace names are hard on speech-to-text, and the mic here plays a script. Real use needs fuzzy name matching, Whisper or a native app, and an undo on everything applied by voice.
Live preview means one dev server per threadEvery thread gets its own worktree and its own port, and they all eat memory. Start a thread's server when you open its live preview and stop it a few minutes after you close it. Also, T3 Code's maintainers mostly take small fixes, so a canvas this different probably lives in your own fork. Plan for rebasing.
Screenshots go staleA capture is only true for the build it came from. Stamp each one with its commit and session, and mark it "outdated" once that milestone moves on, or you'll judge today's work by yesterday's picture. These are drawn stand-ins; the real ones need the tester to save Playwright shots somewhere this page can read (the artifact's file store).
Hiding tickets has a costWhen a milestone stalls, you'll want to see why. Keep the tickets one click away inside the current milestone, even if they're never on the master.

C3 · One window: the monitor composes it, you set the dial

night · generative composition · T3 Code canvas

This replaces the chat canvas. Modes are per session: each session chip shows its mode, and its chat drawer has the dial. The workspace page is the sum of its sessions. Autopilot sessions add nothing, Tell me adds a brief card, Grill me adds its questions, and Jam adds the live board and chat. Blocked or irreversible items surface whatever the mode. On the left is the front page. A small monitor agent (Haiku-class) keeps it current, waking on events rather than polling. It groups the workspaces as Needs you, Jamming, Moving and Quiet. "Needs you" is pinned by rule, never by the model's judgment. On the right is the workspace you picked, laid out by its mode. Autopilot shows the engine, the calls it made alone, and only what's blocked. Tell me shows a brief. Grill me asks you questions before the next milestone. Jam puts the open design call up as live apps you can pin comments on. Flip the dial on any workspace and the monitor recomposes it from the same block kit, so nothing is free-form HTML. "{ } spec" shows exactly what it emitted. The bar at the bottom always talks to whatever's open. Files, skills and plugins move to settings.

Walk me throughThe button at the top runs a 9-step tour on omxcore: three sessions, an auto-routed message, a pinned one that finishes a milestone, and the mode dial changing the page.
The journey mapAt the top of every workspace is a small hand-drawn statechart. It starts at a little house, runs through milestone stickers (mint when done, yellow for where you are, dashed for what's ahead), and ends at a flag with the goal on it. A robot stands on the current milestone with a bubble: ! blocked, ? it's asking you, ♪ jamming, … working. Jams float as clouds over their milestone. ▶ replays the trip, and when a milestone lands the robot hops forward by itself. Click a sticker to open that milestone's tickets.
Milestones hold the ticketsEvery mode shows the plan as a plain checklist: milestones in order down to the goal, and inside each one just the ticket names with a mark. Green ✓ done, ● in progress, ‖ stopped (with why), ✕ red, ○ to do. The current milestone is open and the rest fold. Locking a design in Jam, or finishing Grill me, writes tickets straight into it, tagged jam or grill. Typing "add …" in the bar adds one too.
New and archived workspacesThe floating + opens a short form: name, where it should end up, where it starts from (fresh, existing repo, fork), how much of you it needs, and optional milestones. Or just say "new workspace pantry, goal: …". Archive is on every workspace header and on the Quiet cards. It stops the monitor and the dev server, keeps the worktree, branch and session history, and moves the workspace to Archived at the bottom, where Restore brings it back.
What's fixed, what's generatedThe skeleton is fixed: front page left, workspace right, bar at the bottom. Each workspace's insides are generated: the monitor picks blocks from a kit (track, status, engine, made, interrupt, brief, capture, grill, design, chat) and writes the prose. Status values come from data, never from the model.
In T3 CodeThe monitor subscribes to T3's event log (turn done, session end, test failed) and writes a validated view spec per workspace. The React client renders it, and an unknown block falls back to C2. Jam previews are each thread's previewUrl. Pins ask the page what's under the cursor and go to the thread as feedback.

Cons to look into

A moving interface erodes trustIf the monitor reshuffles blocks every hour, you stop knowing where to look. Here, only a mode change or a new design call recomposes the view. Keep it that way.
Haiku will misjudge sometimesIt's fine for prose and ordering, but it'll call something unimportant that wasn't. Red, blocked and irreversible items surface by rule, and the spec view lets you check why something was shown.
Grill me can nagCap it at three questions per milestone, and let "ask me tomorrow" be a real answer.
Pins on a live page driftA pin knows which element it was on, but if the developer renames or moves that element, old feedback points at nothing. Store the element, the route and a cropped screenshot together.
Cost of always-onA monitor that recomposes on every event across 10 workspaces adds up. Batch events, recompose only the workspace that changed, and keep the front page prose under one line each.
Fork weightThis replaces T3 Code's main canvas. Build it as a separate route (/window) with the block kit in its own package, so pulling upstream changes doesn't mean redoing the canvas.

How answering works, in every option

One tapEach decision lists its options as pills. A tap saves the pick on the page, and the next digest run reads it and acts. You never type unless you want to.
Comment for the rest"Comment" opens the artifact's own comment thread on that card. The digest job passes the text to the workspace's thread.
Reversible stays quietAgents only put calls in the decision log. You see the log when you open a row; nothing in it asks for an answer.
Fits a laptopDesktop screens fit 1440 × 900 with nothing to scroll. The table scrolls inside its card once there are more than about 12 workspaces.
